Page references below are **1-based PDF file pages**. Values read from graphs are approximate. A slide's age, a rounded equivalent, a hypothetical example, or an exercise omitted from the current draft is not, by itself, evidence of an outdated number. This checks displayed results against the supplied drafts; it does not rerun the underlying empirical or simulation code.

### Comparison details

**Wage erosion and conflict costs.** The transitory-inflation welfare impact also changes from −0.95% to −0.93%. For persistent inflation, the real-wage component changes from −1.16% to −1.32%, and the conflict component from −2.15% to −1.91%. These are the corresponding inflation experiments and annual-consumption units, not rounding differences. Both calibration and welfare tables were visually checked.

**Bankruptcy resolution.** The validation plot on slide page 59 and Figure IA1 in the current draft both compare the World Bank bankruptcy-efficiency measure with a recovery proxy constructed from loan impairments and nonperforming loans. The slide additionally says it removes year fixed effects. Its old sample description and empirical scatter do not reproduce the updated draft figure; no other current-draft validation figure reproduces the slide.

The contraction plot on slide page 34 also differs from current draft Figure IA10, Panel B, on page 48: five-year responses are approximately +2/−0.9 percentage points in the slide and +5/−2.1 in the draft for low/high bankruptcy efficiency. **This is not treated as an independent, unqualified numerical error:** the slide values remain consistent with Table IA15 on page 66, while Figure IA10 describes additional investment-growth controls. That specification distinction matters. The removal decision rests on the updated recovery-rate validation evidence above.

**Deficit financing.** The comparison is between the slide's “Quantitative Relevance of Self-financing” figure and the draft's baseline quantitative Figure 3, with the same coefficient labels and output/inflation/self-financing panels. The draft normalizes the initial deficit to one percent of steady-state output. The changes are not a simple proportional rescaling: the self-financing share and relative output/inflation magnitudes also differ. Rendered figures were inspected together.

**Future consumption and MPCs.** The gradual-uncertainty series also changes from approximately 1.68 to 1.58 at λ ≈ 0.622. The axes, benchmark and exercise title correspond. The slide gives no separate calibration explaining its different series. The deck's additional lottery exercise and approximately 0.18 deliberate-MPC claim are absent from the current draft; absence alone was not used as a reason to remove the link.
